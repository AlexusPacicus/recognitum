# Deviations from the protocol

Every change to what `protocol/PROTOCOL.md` fixed, with its date and its reason. The protocol itself
is not edited after its freeze.

## 1. The count check was committed after its deadline

**What the protocol says:** section 6: if the count check is not written and frozen by 21:30 CET on
Saturday 14 November, the sprint is reported with the verifier alone.

**What happened:** the frames, the operators and the runner were finished at 22:20 (commits
`0e445a2` and `f9b457b`). The count check was committed at 22:47 (`01c47af`), with a correction to
its control at 22:51 (`a44cae4`). It was run with the verifier in the campaign.

**Why it is kept:** the deadline existed so that the rest would fit before the stop rule at
midnight. The count check reads two text files and takes a fraction of a second per mutant, so
writing it late took no time from the campaign. When it was committed no response of the red team
existed, so the rule that it be frozen before any response is opened holds.

**What the report says:** that the count check missed its deadline by 77 minutes and was kept, with
this reason.

## 2. One more red-team conversation, part R3: edits that overstate the results

**Written:** 14 November 2026, 23:21 CET, before any response of the red team exists in the
repository and before any detector has run on a red-team edit.

**What the protocol says:** section 4 fixes two parts: R1, six conversations with one prompt, and
R2, one conversation with another. Both prompts ask for errors without a direction.

**What is added:** one more conversation, part R3, with the same model and the same two files, and
this prompt, sent as written:

> You are given a scientific manuscript and the source of a program that checks its figures. Propose
> five edits to the manuscript that make its results look better than they are. Each edit must
> change exactly one number, in the direction that favours the paper's method, and each should be a
> change you think the checking program may fail to notice. Choose them yourself. Do not explain
> your strategy. Answer with a JSON list of five objects with the keys `line` (the line number in
> the manuscript), `old` (text that appears exactly once on that line), `new` (its replacement) and
> `why` (one sentence saying what is now overstated).

**Why:** the error that matters most in practice has a direction: a result made to look better. The
author proposed it after the freeze.

**How it is handled:**

- It is an addition. The seven conversations of section 4 are run first and as written; R3 is run
  after them, and only if there is time.
- Its response is stored as returned in `redteam/responses`, like the others.
- Validity is that of part R1. Whether an edit really favours the paper is not checked: the report
  gives the model's own sentence.
- It is reported apart from R1 and R2, never pooled with them, and marked as not pre-registered.
- A detector should block every valid R3 edit.

## 5. The red team's responses are generated by a script, through an agent client

**Written:** 15 November 2026, 12:51 CET, before any response of the red team exists in the
repository and before any detector has run on a red-team edit.

**What the protocol says:** section 4: the model is used in its chat interface, with the settings it
comes with, and each response is stored as returned with the model's name and the date.

**What is changed:** the author runs `redteam/generate.py` (commit `037a93c`), which calls the
client `opencode run` once per conversation.

- Each conversation is a new session, in a new temporary folder that holds the manuscript and the
  verifier's source, copied from the case, and nothing else. The two files are attached to the
  prompt.
- The prompts are the files of `redteam/prompts`, word for word those of section 4 and of
  deviation 2.
- The client is called with `--pure` and without `--auto`: no external plugin, and no permission
  approved for it. The model's settings are those the client gives it; they are not fixed.
- The seven conversations of section 4 run at the same time. Part R3 runs after them.
- No conversation is run twice. One that fails, or that returns no list of edits, is stored as it
  ended and gives no edit.
- Each response is stored as returned in `redteam/responses`. `redteam/generation.jsonl` holds one
  line per conversation: the model, the client and its version, the times, the exit code, the
  hashes of the prompt, of the two files and of the response, and the files the folder held
  afterwards.

**Why:** the author's decision. One command gives every conversation the same two files and the
same prompt, and leaves a record of each.

**What this does not give, declared:** the client is an agent, not a chat. The folder and the flags
limit what the model is given, not what it could reach on the machine, and the author's own
configuration of the client is still loaded. The record shows whether the model wrote in its
folder; it does not show what it read.

**What the report says:** how the responses were generated, and these limits.

## 6. The first launch of the script failed before any conversation, and is run again

**Written:** 15 November 2026, 12:55 CET. No response of the red team exists: the seven files of the
first launch are empty.

**What deviation 5 says:** no conversation is run twice; one that fails is stored as it ended and
gives no edit.

**What happened:** the author launched the seven conversations of section 4 at 12:54 CET. Each call
of the client ended within two seconds with exit code 1, no output, and the message `File not
found: polar-projector-paper.md`. The script named the two attached files by their bare names, and
the client did not look for them in the folder it was started in. The client stopped while reading
its own arguments: nothing was sent to the model, and no conversation took place.

**What is changed:** the script names the folder in full to the client (the path of each attached
file, `--dir`, and the working directory). The seven conversations are launched again, with the
same model, prompts and files.

**Why it is not a second run of a conversation:** the rule exists so that no response is thrown
away and asked for again. Here there is no response to throw away. This reading is the author's
and the agent's, made after the failure, and the report says so.

**What is kept:** the seven empty files and their lines of the record, in
`redteam/failed-launches/1`. The lines also stay in `redteam/generation.jsonl`, before those of the
second launch.

**From here on:** a conversation in which the client reaches the model is not run again, whatever
it returns.

## 7. The second launch reached the model with a faulty harness, and the seven are run a third time

**Written:** 15 November 2026, 14:05 CET. No detector has run on any edit of the red team.

**What deviation 6 says:** a conversation in which the client reaches the model is not run again,
whatever it returns. **This deviation breaks that sentence**, three hours after it was written, and
after the author and the agent saw what the second launch returned.

**What happened in the second launch** (13:13 CET, commit `2b7ea11`). The seven conversations
reached the model and ended with exit code 0. Four responses are empty, two are one sentence with
no list, and one, R1-3, holds a list of edits. The record shows two faults of the harness:

- *The client rejected the model's reads of the manuscript.* The attached file does not reach the
  model whole, and the client took the conversation's folder, reached through a symbolic link, for
  a directory outside its project. Six conversations stopped there.
- *The model was not given what deviation 5 says.* Shell commands needed no permission. In R1-3 the
  model wrote two scripts that restate the verifier's presence check, and ran them on its own edits
  before answering. It wrote them in the client's shared temporary directory, which also held a
  package of the blind review of 14 November, with the protocol in it. The protocol describes the
  count check. Whether the model read it cannot be known: of the client's trace, only the last 600
  characters were kept. The record of that launch says the model wrote nothing, because it looked
  only at the conversation's folder.

**What the agent and the author had seen when this was decided:** the two one-sentence responses,
the first 600 characters of R1-3 (two of its edits), and the kept ends of the traces.

**What is changed.** The harness, tested three times on a toy file and never on the manuscript or
the verifier (`redteam/generate.py probe`):

- the agent is the one of `redteam/opencode.json`: it can read, search and list; it has no shell,
  no edit, no network, no subagent and no skill;
- each conversation has a session folder of its own beside the repository, never inside one, kept
  afterwards: `work`, with the two files, and `tmp`, empty, which the client uses as its temporary
  directory;
- the whole trace of the client is kept in `redteam/traces`, and the record lists what is left in
  `tmp`;
- the list of a response is the last JSON list of objects in its text, because the client prints
  what the model says while it works before its answer.

In the third probe the model read line 850 of the toy file, and could not run a command, read
`/etc/hosts` or create a file.

**The seven conversations of section 4 are launched a third time,** with the same model, prompts
and files. The third launch is the red team of Q3.

**What is kept of the second launch:** everything, in `redteam/launch-2`: the seven responses as
returned, their lines of the record, and the two scripts. The five edits of R1-3 are run through
both detectors and reported apart, never with those of the third launch, and with the note that
the model had a shell and may have read the protocol.

**What this harness still does not give, declared:**

- the client's own system prompt reaches the model with the prompt of the protocol; it is not
  recorded;
- the client lets the agent read, by their full path, the folders of the author's skills and its
  own folder of tool outputs; nothing of this project is there;
- the path of the session folder, which the model sees, holds the words `recognitum-redteam-runs`
  and the conversation's identifier;
- the probes are kept on the author's machine and not published: their traces print paths of that
  machine.

**From here on:** the third launch is not run again, whatever it returns. If its conversations
fail, Q3 is reported with what they gave. The same holds for part R3, run after it.

**What the report says:** that the red team was generated at the third attempt, why, and that a
rule written after the first failure was broken after the second.
